# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This project is a place to do careful technical work. Everyone who participates — contributors, implementers, translators, and reviewers — is expected to treat others with respect, assume good faith, and argue about the work rather than the person.

## Expected behaviour

- Critique the proposal, the code, and the file, not the contributor.
- Say what evidence would change your mind. "It's wrong" is not a review comment.
- Be direct and be kind; those are not in tension. A precise objection is kinder than vague agreement.
- Accept that maintainers may decide against you, and ask for the reasoning if it is missing.
- Respect that many participants are not native English speakers: the reference documentation is English, and the README is also published in Chinese.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption in any project space.
- Personal attacks, insults, or derogatory comments about a person's identity, background, or ability.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without permission.
- Deliberately derailing a technical discussion, or repeatedly re-litigating a decided question without new evidence.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a problem.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, reviews, commit messages, RFC discussions — and anywhere someone is representing the project in public.

A placeholder address in a published Code of Conduct is worse than none, because it silently swallows reports.

Reports go to the maintainers, who will acknowledge receipt, keep the report confidential, and tell the reporter what happens next. Conflicts of interest are handled by recusal: a maintainer named in a report takes no part in deciding it.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take any action they judge proportionate: a private word, a public correction, a temporary block on a thread, a temporary ban, or a permanent ban from the project's spaces. Decisions are recorded privately, and the reporter is told the outcome.

Enforcement decisions are made by maintainers who are not involved in the incident. A decision can be appealed once, in writing, to the steward named in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).
